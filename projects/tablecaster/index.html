Tablecaster.

Projects tournament battlefield layouts true-to-scale onto a 60″ × 44″ table. Pick the layout, point a projector at the mat, tap the four corners once, and every terrain area, deployment zone and objective lands where it belongs. No tape measure.

Web· Projector· Offline· Free
Open Tablecaster Unofficial fan tool

What it does

Setting up a tournament table means measuring every terrain area, deployment zone and objective from a diagram, for every layout, on every table. Tablecaster draws the layout for you, at 1:1 scale, straight onto the battlefield. Put your terrain on the projected footprints, place the objectives on their markers, and play.

How it works at the table

  • Pick a layout. Choose your force disposition and your opponent's, in either order, then layout A, B or C. Small thumbnails show each one; Random lets chance decide.
  • Calibrate once. In fullscreen, tap the four corners of the mat and check three 10″ bars with a ruler. After that one projected inch is one real inch, and the result is saved for that screen.
  • Project only what you need. Eight layers (grid, zones, territory line, terrain areas, terrain features, objectives, edge labels, background) switch on and off. Hide the zones once deployment is done.
  • Swap sides or mirror. Turn the layout 180° without moving seats, or mirror it for rear and ceiling projection. Neither breaks the calibration.
  • Share the game. The link carries the layout, sides, layers and background. Calibration and mirror stay on each device.

What you need

  • A projector above the table, or on a wall for display.
  • A phone with screen mirroring, or a laptop. On a laptop with two displays, a separate projector window keeps the controls on your screen and only the battlefield on the table.
  • One earlier visit on game day. After that the tool, every layout and the drawn backgrounds work without a signal.

Backgrounds

Pick none, a drawn texture, or an image from your own device. Your own image stays on your device: it is never uploaded and never put in a shared link.

All backgrounds are original: drawn in code or photographed by me, made without generative AI.
